## The gaps

**11. Fonts for other scripts.** The interface and the editor embed Inter and JetBrains Mono (Latin, Greek, Cyrillic) behind egui's own fallback faces. XML documents routinely contain CJK, Arabic, Hebrew and Indic text, and those characters have no face yet. The plan is PdfCraft's approach: one font already installed on the user's machine, read at run time as the last fallback (never embedded or shipped, AGENTS.md §1.4), with its glyphs moved onto Inter's baseline. Measured by a glyph-coverage test and a screenshot with synthetic multi-script text.

**12. Tab strip.** Tabs share the room (at least 150 px each), then the strip scrolls, the wheel scrolls it sideways and the shown tab is brought into view; a tab is dragged to a new place, and Move Tab Left/Right (Ctrl+Shift+PageUp/PageDown) reorders it by keyboard (M10.2, `ui-egui/tests/tabs.rs`). Left: tab previews.
**17. Incremental analysis.** Every edit asks for a full parse of the text on a worker thread (only the newest request per document is kept). Until the answer arrives the window shows the previous result, and its underlines are clamped to the text rather than moved through the edits made since, so a squiggle can sit beside the wrong word for a moment. A document over 32 MB is not analysed at all. Large-file mode and an incremental reparse (M1.15, driven by `cargo xtask bench`) close it; measured by the keystroke-to-paint and reparse targets in `docs/target-app-parity.md`.

**18. Colouring far into a very large document.** The colour of a line depends on where the lexer is at its start (inside a comment, a tag, a value), which is known only by reading everything before it; the first jump to the end of a 100 MB document used to read it all (1.2 s against the target of 16 ms). Above 1 MiB the highlighter now starts a far place from a guess: it runs the lexer over the 256 KiB before the line (counted in bytes, from the first line that starts inside that, so a very long line cannot widen it), from the state of content. That finds everything the lexer knows that begins in the window: a comment, a CDATA section, a processing instruction, a tag, an attribute value or a declaration that goes over lines, and what a comment inside a DOCTYPE returns to. For a text that fits the window it is exact (property test `estimating_in_a_text_that_fits_the_window_is_exact_whatever_goes_over_lines`). What is left: a construct that began before the window and has not ended (a comment or CDATA section of more than 256 KiB, say) is not seen, and the lines in it are coloured as content until it ends, and then they are right again. The first jump is 0.9 ms for 100 MB (`scroll_100mb_far`, budget 16 ms). The other half of large-file mode is not done: the outline and the validation of a document over 32 MB are not streamed (gap 17), and the view finds the width of a document for its horizontal scroll in one pass up to 256 MB (45 ms for 100 MB, `widest_line_100mb`) and by the lines the carets visit beyond that.

**19. Content-model automata.** `xmlcraft-grammar` (M2.5) compiles a content model to a Glushkov automaton that steps, says what may come next, finishes the shortest way and checks determinism, and agrees with a reference matcher on random models. Three things are missing for XML Schema: a counted occurrence (`maxOccurs="1000"`) is unrolled into copies, so a model over 100,000 positions (or one whose follow lists would be huge, such as 50,000 optional copies of an optional element) is refused, where XSDs in the wild sometimes use `maxOccurs="100000"` or more; the `all` group (each child at most once, in any order) is not a particle yet (the XSD compiler matches it with slots of its own, `xmlcraft-xsd`'s `ContentModel`); and `mandatory` in `expected` is worked out only where there are at most 32 different things to choose from. Counters instead of unrolling and an exact `mandatory` are still to do (M3.7 compiled `all` with slots of its own; a model over 100,000 positions is reported as `xsd.limit`). Measured by the reference test in `crates/grammar/tests/reference.rs` and by xsdtests from M3.

**20. Entry Helpers.** The pane (M2.11) lists, from the DTD of the DOCTYPE, the elements the content model allows after the children that come before the caret, the attributes of the element the caret is in and the entities. Two things are missing. The list looks only at what comes before the caret: an element that fits there may still make what follows invalid (an element is offered between two siblings without asking whether the second would still be accepted). The DTD is read when the DOCTYPE (or the document's place) changes, not when a file it names changes on disk, and it is not cached between documents. The popup in the editor while typing (`<`, `</`, a space in a tag, `="`, `&`) completes the names the document itself uses, Edit ▸ Complete (⌘I, Ctrl+I) asks for it, and the grammar's elements, attributes, entities and values (M1.10d). A document with no DOCTYPE that names an XML Schema is answered from it (M3.12), the element at the caret typed by walking down from the root through the content models; there the names under a wildcard, the members of an abstract type and what `xs:redefine` redefines are not offered. Measured by `crates/complete/tests/choices.rs`, `crates/ui-egui/tests/entry_helpers.rs` and `crates/automation/tests/entry_helpers.rs`.

**21. The XSD engine.** `xmlcraft-xsd` reads a schema document (M3.3), follows includes and imports (M3.4), assembles the components with derivation, groups, wildcards, substitution groups, defaults and identity constraints (M3.5, M3.8), compiles the content models and checks Unique Particle Attribution and Element Declarations Consistent (M3.7); `xmlcraft-validate` validates a document against the result (M3.9, first part). The whole schema for schemas assembles without a diagnostic and validates the bundled schemas. The tool `xml_validate` (and `xmlcraft-cli validate --schema FILE`) validates against a given schema or the one `xsi:schemaLocation` names, loaded through the resolver (M3.15, first part). F8 in the app uses it too (M3.15, second part). What is missing: (1) the UBL schemas the app ships are known to its catalog only by their namespaces, so Assign Schema lists them but cannot name them (a UBL document finds its schema without naming it; the W3C ones are written by their public addresses, M4.15); (2) `xs:redefine` is loaded but the redefining definitions are not applied (`xsd.unsupported`); (3) a restriction is not checked against the content model of its base (Derivation Valid (Restriction)), nor is the `final` of a substitution head applied to the types of its members; (4) values in the messages carry the `cvc-*` names only for datatype violations; (5) the elements under a wildcard that is skipped or lax have no type, so a field of an identity constraint finds no value in them; (6) the W3C XML Schema Test Suite (M3.10, `cargo xtask conformance xsdtests`) is run and ratcheted: **98.23%** of its 38,438 accepted tests (schemas 96.21%, instances 99.34%; the NIST datatype tests 100%). The 682 known failures are in `xtask/baselines/xsdtests.txt`; by their message, 435 are schemas or instances that should be refused and are not (the schema constraints of Derivation Valid, and the like), 54 are Unique Particle Attribution reported where the suite says there is none, 45 are wildcards (an element or attribute a wildcard lets in is not found), 23 are content models over the size limit, about 50 are `gMonth` values and facets, and about 30 are pattern syntax (`\p{…}` categories, character ranges in classes); (7) one test group, `schZ012_a`, is left out of the count: it asks for a document by a name that differs in case from the file's (`schZ012_A.xsd`), which is that file on a Mac or on Windows and no file on Linux, so its verdict is the file system's. (A document that goes by two names, a link or the other case on a file system that folds case, is read once: the loader knows a file by its real path.) Also measured by `crates/xsd/tests` and `crates/validate/tests`.

**22. Web formats.** HTML5 gets entry helpers and a locked-down browser view (a native webview with JavaScript off and the network blocked unless a document is allowed); OpenAPI 2.0, 3.0, 3.1 and 3.2 are validated against the official JSON Schemas with the version found from `swagger` or `openapi`; WSDL 1.1/2.0 are edited and validated, SOAP 1.1 and 1.2 messages are built, validated and sent by a client, and a debugger is an intercepting proxy that breaks per operation (XPath conditions), lets the message be edited and forwards it. Measured by the loopback mock service tests and the official OpenAPI examples.

**23. Business formats.** XBRL 2.1 instances and taxonomies (discovery, calculation, dimensions, inline XBRL, taxonomy packages, OIM conversions; formula and tables later), and e-invoices: XRechnung (UBL and CII), EN 16931, Peppol BIS Billing 3, FatturaPA, ZUGFeRD/Factur-X (profiles MINIMUM to EXTENDED and XRECHNUNG). The e-invoice formats are not measured against the leading commercial XML editors; they are designed from the public standards. A PDF with embedded XML is edited by an incremental update only, so the bytes of the original revision stay unchanged (and a signature still verifies that revision); an edit does make the file differ from what an approval signature covered, so the preflight check reads the signature and permission fields (DocMDP), refuses certified and encrypted files, and warns before editing a signed one. Measured by the KoSIT XRechnung test suite, an XBRL conformance subset and the PDF round-trip tests. Where the e-invoices stand (M9.10–M9.14): the UBL 2.1 and CII D16B schemas, the CEN EN 16931 Schematron 1.3.17 (UBL and CII) and the XRechnung Schematron 2.6.0 ship with the app, and `einvoice_validate` runs them in stages, offline; every one of the 1,943 assertions of the four rule sets compiles on the engine, and on the synthetic XRechnung fixtures none is left unevaluated (see [target-app-parity.md](target-app-parity.md#e-invoice-rules-en-16931-xrechnung) for the table), and comparisons with NaN follow XPath (the published `xee` answered `NaN ge 0` with true; XmlCraft carries a patched `xee-interpreter` in `vendor/`, M9.12); `einvoice_model` reads UBL and CII into one neutral model (M9.13), and `einvoice_render_pdf` makes a PDF that shows the invoice and embeds its XML byte for byte, as a ZUGFeRD / Factur-X hybrid for CII (M9.14; written with PdfCraft's `pdfcraft-cos`; PDF/A-3 is claimed only when the user gives an ICC output profile, since none ships, and neither PDF/A nor Factur-X conformance is verified with veraPDF or Mustang). Missing: the KoSIT test suite is not run (so agreement with the reference implementation is not measured; the rules the fixtures do not reach are untested), the Peppol BIS Billing 3 rules and the FatturaPA schema are not bundled (their publishers reserve redistribution; the owner's approval stands, consent or another licence is needed), ZUGFeRD/Factur-X profiles below EN 16931 have no rules (MINIMUM, BASIC WL), there is no e-invoice window or Problems integration (the tool only), and a large invoice is slow (300 lines take about 5 s; the time budget is 60 s, after which the checks not reached are reported as not evaluated).

Details are added here as each gap is worked, including known approximations (for example in XSD particle restriction) and the test that measures them. Nothing may be marked closed here without a cited test.
